2. MANRS ACTIONS

ACTION 1: PREVENT PROPAGATION OF INCORRECT ROUTING INFORMATION

Has your organization implemented Action 1?
Yes, we prevent propagation of incorrect routing information.

Describe your implementation of Action 1. (Confidential, Mandatory)

AS218822 operates an IPv6 routing service using BIRD 2. We maintain explicit
prefix and AS-path allowlists in version control following manual review of
authoritative RIR records, IRR route6 objects, and RPKI origin authorizations.
We do not use an automatic IRR-to-filter generator.

The core originates only 2a06:9801:ff0::/44. Customer propagation is restricted
to AS201423's exact prefixes, 2a0f:6284:b::/48 and 2a0f:6284:c::/48. Their AS
paths must consist solely of AS201423, with prepending permitted, and their
origins must be RPKI Valid. RPKI Invalid and NotFound customer routes are rejected.

External exports are default-deny. Customer routes must arrive through the
approved internal routing path before external export. The GRE and Vultr edges
export only the aggregate received from the core. Unrelated transit routes
cannot be re-exported as our own or approved customer announcements.

Transit imports apply prefix-length and AS-path sanity checks and reject
defaults, local address space, specified special-purpose prefixes,
reserved/private ASNs, and RPKI Invalid routes.

Adjacent operators can identify our intended announcements through our public
routing policy, RIPE aut-num and route6 objects, and AS set
RIPE::AS218822:AS-PMHONLY.

How frequently are these filters updated? (Confidential, Mandatory)

Our policy requires allowlists to be updated whenever authorized prefixes,
customer relationships, or interconnections change, before the corresponding
announcements are enabled. The documented review cadence for registry data,
authorization records, and existing allowlists is monthly.

RPKI updates are handled separately through an RTR cache. The router's RTR
refresh interval is configured to 600 seconds, and BGP import/export tables
support automatic filter revalidation when the ROA table changes. This does not
automatically regenerate the manually maintained IRR-derived allowlists.

What mechanism is used to check that a customer legitimately holds the ASN and
IP blocks they intend to announce? (Confidential, Mandatory)

Our documented onboarding procedure requires checking the customer's ASN
assignment and address allocation or delegation in the authoritative RIR
database, verifying the corresponding IRR route6 origin, and checking valid
ROA coverage for each intended prefix and origin ASN.

The requester's authority must also be confirmed through an independently
obtained registered operational contact or an authenticated resource-maintainer
channel. Dated authorization or an LOA, together with the reviewer's identity,
must be retained privately.

A BGP session, tunnel key, or PeeringDB entry alone is not accepted as proof of
authority. Only explicitly approved prefixes and AS paths are enabled;
additional prefixes or downstream ASNs require another review.

Other implementation details (Confidential, Optional)

The customer session has a two-route import limit and a ten-route receive limit,
with disable actions on excess. CI validates BIRD configuration and tests
rejection of incorrect prefixes, unauthorized AS paths, and routes without
valid origin authorization. Deployment is performed through GitOps.

Implementation documentation:
https://github.com/pmh-only/AS218822/blob/main/routing/MANRS.md

AS218822 is newly established. RIPE RIS records its first visibility on
September 1, 2026, so it has not yet completed the standard three-month global
routing visibility period. Please advise whether an early review or an
exception is possible.

ACTION 2: PREVENT TRAFFIC WITH SPOOFED SOURCE IP ADDRESSES

Has your organization implemented Action 2?
Yes, we prevent traffic with spoofed source IP addresses.

Link to CAIDA Spoofer Results:
https://spoofer.caida.org/report.php?sessionid=2229273

Describe your implementation of Action 2. (Confidential)

Our deployed core, GRE gateway, and Vultr edge apply source-address validation
using explicit interface and prefix policies.

Customer WireGuard ingress is restricted to the customer's authorized source
prefixes and exact peering address. Corresponding nftables checks protect both
router input and forwarding. Tailscale end-user access uses authenticated peers
and additional tailnet source-prefix checks.

External ingress rejects packets claiming sources from the locally routed
infrastructure prefix 2a06:9801:ff0::/48 or our Tailscale IPv6 prefix. Legitimate
internal, multihomed-customer, and separately routed cloud traffic is preserved.

External routing-tunnel egress is restricted to our owned aggregate and approved
customer prefixes. Provider transport addresses are restricted to
router-originated traffic. Egress validation runs after source NAT, and scoped
IPv6 control-traffic exceptions preserve neighbor discovery and
multicast-listener operation.

Following remediation of an ingress-filtering gap, we ran CAIDA Spoofer on
September 8, 2026 from two separate routed, non-NAT IPv6 measurement segments
behind the core: 2a06:9801:ff0:100::/64 and 2a06:9801:ff0:101::/64. Public
anonymized sharing was enabled.

The successful post-remediation reports are:

https://spoofer.caida.org/report.php?sessionid=2229220

https://spoofer.caida.org/report.php?sessionid=2229273

CAIDA attributes both to AS218822 and reports no NAT. Both clients received
complete server summaries confirming blocked outbound private and unrelated
routable sources, and blocked inbound private and forged internal sources.
Both exited successfully.

Validation is prefix-based: each measurement peer is authorized to use its
assigned /64. AS218822 does not originate IPv4 prefixes, and these AS218822
measurements cover IPv6. Please confirm how the application's IPv4 testing
requirement should be handled for this IPv6-only routing service.

Comment on Action 2 (Public, Mandatory)

AS218822 enforces source-prefix validation at customer boundaries, rejects
forged local sources arriving from transit, and validates external
routing-tunnel egress. Public CAIDA reports document successful IPv6
anti-spoofing measurements from two non-NAT routed segments.
